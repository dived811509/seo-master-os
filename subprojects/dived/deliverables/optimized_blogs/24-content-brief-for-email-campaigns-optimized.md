# Email Campaign Content Brief Template: How to Write Briefs That Work

Requesting an email with vague directions like *"Write an email for our weekend sale"* inevitably leads to multiple revision rounds, delayed sends, and mismatched expectations. Utilizing a structured **email campaign content brief template** eliminates guesswork before copywriting and design begin.

**Quick answer:** A complete **email campaign brief** defines six core details: strategic goal, target segment, single key message, required assets (offers/links/codes), tone guidance, and a single primary call to action (CTA).

Standardizing your briefing process using a reusable **email brief template** speeds up team execution while ensuring campaign alignment across copywriters, designers, and managers.

## What Is an Email Content Brief?

An email content brief is a concise planning document providing copywriters, designers, and platform managers with everything necessary to create an accurate email campaign on the first attempt.

Rather than relying on informal verbal requests, a formal brief spells out campaign objectives, offer terms, target audience segments, and required legal disclaimers upfront.

## Why Vague Briefs Drain Time and Budget

Informal requests shift missing information into endless revision loops. The short time spent writing a thorough brief upfront saves hours lost to draft rewrites.

A clear brief ensures that copywriters focus on messaging, designers create matching visuals, and platform managers configure correct segment filters without back-and-forth friction.

## Core Elements Every Email Campaign Brief Requires

Regardless of campaign scope, every **email campaign brief** must specify:

1. **Strategic Goal:** The measurable objective (e.g., *"Drive repeat purchases of category X"*, not just *"Promote sale"*).
2. **Target Audience / Segment:** The exact recipient list (e.g., *"Past buyers who haven't ordered in 60 days"* versus *"Full database"*).
3. **Primary Key Message:** The single core take-away stated in one clear sentence.
4. **Required Assets & Details:** Specific promo codes, discount percentages, landing page URLs, start/end dates, and disclaimers.
5. **Tone Guidance:** Desired brand voice for the send (e.g., *"Urgent and direct"* vs. *"Warm and conversational"*).
6. **Primary Call to Action (CTA):** The specific destination link and button copy (e.g., *"Shop Collection X"*).

## What to Omit From a Content Brief

A brief should outline objectives and parameters without over-dictating exact sentence phrasing. Overly prescriptive briefs stifle creative execution, producing stiff, checklist-like copy.

Define the strategic boundaries and necessary details, giving copywriters and designers space to craft compelling creative concepts.

## Reusable Email Campaign Content Brief Template

Copy and adapt this standardized framework for your team's campaigns:

```markdown
# EMAIL CAMPAIGN BRIEF: [Campaign Name]

- Send Date: [Date]
- Target Audience / Segment: [e.g., VIP Customers, Abandoned Carts]
- Strategic Goal: [Primary business objective]
- Key Message: [Single sentence core takeaway]

## Campaign Parameters
- Offer Details / Promo Code: [e.g., 20% off with code SAVE20]
- Key Dates / Expiration: [e.g., Ends Sunday midnight]
- Primary CTA & URL: [e.g., Button: "Shop New Arrivals" -> Link]
- Tone Guidance: [e.g., Upbeat, seasonal, high-urgency]

## Required Copy & Design Assets
- Header Visuals: [e.g., Product collage showcasing collection]
- Specific Copy Inclusions: [e.g., Free shipping threshold note]
```

## Briefing Automated Flows vs. One-Off Campaigns

Briefing emails for automated workflows (like welcome or cart recovery series) requires one extra parameter: specifying where the email sits within the broader sequence.

Detailing what preceding emails cover prevents repeating identical points or missing key setup context for subsequent sends.

## Briefing A/B Tests Requires Hypothesis Testing

When briefing an A/B test campaign, explicitly state the variable tested and the underlying hypothesis:

- **Testing Variable:** Subject line directness (Version A: Direct Offer vs. Version B: Curiosity).
- **Hypothesis:** Direct offer subject lines will yield higher open rates for unengaged segments.
- **Success Metric:** Open rate for subject lines; Click-Through Rate for CTA tests.

Documenting hypotheses ensures team members draw meaningful conclusions from test results.
