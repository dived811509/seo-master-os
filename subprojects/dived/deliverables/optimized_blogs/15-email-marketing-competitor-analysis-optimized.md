# How to Do an Ecommerce Email Marketing Competitor Analysis

You can guess at what your competitors are doing in email, or you can just sign up for their list and watch. Running a thorough **ecommerce email marketing competitor analysis** is one of the few pieces of competitive research that costs nothing but a spare inbox and a bit of patience, yet most store owners never bother doing it properly.

**Quick answer:** Run an **email marketing competitor analysis** by subscribing to 3 to 5 competitor lists, tracking send frequency, subject lines, design layouts, and promotional offers for several weeks. Compare findings against your own program to spot messaging gaps and quiet calendar windows.

## Pick the Right Competitors to Track

Signing up for every brand vaguely related to your product category creates more noise than insight. A tighter list of 3 to 5 competitors works far better:

- **2 to 3 direct competitors** selling similar products to a similar target audience on platforms like Shopify or WooCommerce.
- **1 to 2 aspirational brands** known for excellent email marketing execution, even if they aren't direct competitors, since strong execution is worth studying regardless of category.

Set up a dedicated email inbox specifically for **competitor email tracking** so your primary personal or work inbox doesn't get cluttered with promotional mail.

## What to Actually Track

Once you're subscribed, the value comes from tracking specific, comparable details over time rather than just skimming individual emails as they land in your inbox.

- **Send frequency:** how many emails per week, and whether that changes around sales periods or product drops.
- **Subject line style:** direct and benefit-led, curiosity-driven, emoji usage, or personalization tokens.
- **Email content mix:** the balance of promotional offers, educational tips, user-generated content, and transactional updates.
- **Design and length:** image-heavy templates versus plain-text styles, short scannable copy versus long-form story emails.
- **Offers and urgency tactics:** discount depth, countdown timers, limited-stock messaging, or free shipping thresholds.
- **Landing pages:** where each email's primary call to action sends the reader, and how closely the landing page mirrors the email promise.

Recording these observations in a simple spreadsheet, one row per email, makes patterns visible after a few weeks that are easy to miss when reading emails individually.

## Use a Simple Framework, Not Just Raw Notes

Raw notes about competitor emails are interesting but hard to execute without structure. A basic SWOT-style framework works well here: for each competitor, note where their email program is clearly stronger than yours, where it's weaker, what opportunities their content gaps create for you, and what threats their strengths pose if you don't respond.

Even a simple two-column list, "things they do better" and "things we do better", across your tracked competitors usually surfaces two or three actionable ideas within the first month. Framing it as a search for blind spots, specifically what your competitors are *not* doing that your brand could own, yields sharper strategic wins than a generic overview.

## Plot Timing on an Actual Calendar

Beyond frequency counts, physically plotting the days and times each competitor's emails land in a calendar view reveals patterns a spreadsheet of numbers alone tends to hide. You might notice a competitor consistently sends on Tuesday and Thursday mornings, or that everyone in your category blasts emails the moment a sale starts and goes quiet for a week after.

This visual timeline makes two things obvious fast: whether competitors are risking email fatigue by over-sending, and whether there's a genuinely quiet window in the calendar your store could occupy without competing for inbox attention.

## What to Do With What You Find

The point of an ecommerce email marketing competitor analysis isn't to copy someone else's exact approach; a tactic that fits their audience and brand voice might fail for yours. The goal is identifying hypotheses worth testing on your own list.

- If competitors send 3+ emails a week and you send one, test increasing your send frequency gradually using automated flows.
- If competitors lean heavily on aggressive discounts while you don't, consider whether a value-first, educational tone could be your primary differentiator.
- If a competitor's post-purchase flow features clever restock reminders, prototype a similar sequence in your email automation setup.

Treat every finding as an idea to test on your list. Your audience's response is the only metric that confirms whether a tactic works for your brand specifically.

## How Often to Repeat This Analysis

A one-time competitive review goes stale within a few months, as brands pivot strategies, adjust frequency, and launch new campaigns. Revisiting your tracked competitor list once a quarter, or right before major peak seasons like Black Friday / Cyber Monday, keeps your insight fresh.

## Manual Competitor Tracking vs. Dedicated Tools

Everything described above can be done manually with a free email account and a spreadsheet. For most growing stores, manual tracking is genuinely sufficient.

As your tracked list expands past five or six competitors, or if you need historic archives going back years, dedicated **competitor email tracking** tools automatically capture and index competitor email campaigns. Manual tracking forces you to read emails closely, which often yields deeper copywriting insights during early stages.

## Common Mistakes That Waste the Effort

- **Tracking too many competitors at once.** Beyond 5 or 6, volume becomes overwhelming. Depth beats breadth.
- **Only analyzing subject lines.** Subject lines drive opens, but body copy, visual design, and landing pages determine conversions.
- **Failing to compare findings against your own email data.** Tracking competitors without auditing your own sends side-by-side turns analysis into passive reading.
- **Copying tactics without brand adaptation.** A tactic that works for a brand with a playful voice will feel disjointed if forced into a premium brand position.

## Key Takeaways

- Track 3 to 5 competitors, balancing direct rivals with aspirational email marketing leaders.
- Set up dedicated competitor email tracking in a separate inbox to keep data organized.
- Execute an ecommerce email marketing competitor analysis systematically using a spreadsheet and SWOT framework.
- Plot email send times on a visual calendar to uncover quiet inbox windows.
- Treat competitive insights as hypotheses to test, rather than rules to copy blindly.
- Repeat your audit quarterly to keep track of shifting competitor strategies.

## FAQs

### How many competitors should I track for email analysis?

3 to 5 works best: 2 to 3 direct competitors plus 1 to 2 aspirational brands known for exceptional email marketing.

### What is competitor email tracking?

Competitor email tracking involves subscribing to rival email lists to monitor send frequency, subject lines, design layouts, and promotional offers over time.

### Should I copy what my competitors do in their emails?

No. Treat competitor tactics as hypotheses to test on your list. Audience response is the only reliable validator.

### How often should I update my competitor email audit?

Quarterly is ideal. Competitor strategies shift frequently, especially heading into Q4 shopping seasons.
